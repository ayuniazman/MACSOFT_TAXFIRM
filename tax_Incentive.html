Income Tax Incentive

Overview

A Fundamental Shift in Incentive Design

The NIF, introduced under the National Budget 2026, restructures Malaysia’s investment incentives through a tiered and outcome-based approach. It is guided by two cornerstone national strategies: the National Investment Aspirations (NIA) and the New Industrial Master Plan 2030 (NIMP 2030).

Incentives are granted based on a company’s commitment and performance against the NIA Scorecard, which measures tangible outcomes and impacts on national priorities. The framework is administered by MIDA, with policy oversight by the Ministry of Finance through the Taskforce on Incentive Review (TFIR).

Two Mutually Exclusive Incentives

The Types Of Incentives

01

Special Tax Rate (STR)

A reduced corporate income tax rate on a company’s taxable income for a specified period.

  • Accumulated losses during the STR period can be carried forward for 7 consecutive years and deducted from post-incentive income
  • Approved under Section 65B, Subsection 6(1A), Paragraph 6(1)(m), Schedule 1 Part XVII, Income Tax Act 1967
  • Granted via subsidiary legislation and considered by the National Committee on Investment (NCI)
02

Investment Tax Allowance (ITA)

A capital expenditure-based incentive allowing a company to offset a percentage of its qualifying capital expenditure (QCE) against statutory income.

  • Granted on QCE incurred within a specified period
  • Unutilised allowance can be carried forward until fully utilised
  • Approved under Section 127(3)(b) of the Income Tax Act 1967
  • Considered by the National Committee on Investment (NCI)

Assessment Mechanism

NIA Scorecard and Tiering Approach

1

Advance Tier

Compliance with both minimum and additional conditions entitles the company to the High Performance Tier tax incentive for that particular year of assessment.

2

Standard Tier

Compliance with minimum conditions entitles the company to the Base Tier tax incentive for that particular year of assessment.

Not Eligible: Standard Tax Rate

If the company fails to comply with the minimum conditions, it is not entitled to claim the tax incentive for that year of assessment and will be taxed at prevailing rates.

Outcome Pillars

The NIA Scorecard Pillars

01

Increase Economic Complexity

Assessed through product complexity (Harvard Growth Lab Atlas), R&D expenditure as a percentage of sales revenue, level of technology, and adoption of 4IR technologies such as AI, IoT, big data, and advanced materials.

02

Create High-Valued Job Opportunities

Measured by the percentage of high-skilled workers, median salary per worker, percentage of workers earning RM10,000 and above, and the share of Malaysian workers in Managerial, Technical and Supervisory (MTS) roles.

03

Extend Domestic Linkages

Assessed via the percentage of local input, training expenditure out of total salary, collaboration with local academia and industry, vendor development programmes, and the establishment of regional or global treasury/finance centres conducting cash pooling in Malaysia.

04

Develop New and Existing Industrial Clusters

Considered through patents (filed or registered), alignment with NIA targeted sectors or NIMP 2030 core sectors, and commercialisation of R&D findings from local institutions.

05

Improve Inclusivity

Evaluated by internship and apprenticeship opportunities, fresh graduate employment, the share of women in top management, the share of workers from vulnerable groups, and the percentage of Malaysian workers out of total workers.

06

Enhance Sustainability Practices

Assessed against green manufacturing practices: sustainable materials and services, sustainable waste management, sustainable water consumption, and sustainable energy consumption.

Eligibility

Who Can Apply (Manufacturing Sector)

01

New Company

A company newly incorporated or which has not yet commenced commercial operations, and which does not have any related entity in Malaysia prior to the application — or has a related entity carrying on a different project in Malaysia.

02

Existing Company

A company already operating in Malaysia, carrying on a different project in Malaysia.

Application Process

From Consultation to Compliance

01

Consultation

Engage with MIDA pre-application to ensure the project aligns with the NIF.

02

Submission

Submit a complete application via the InvestMalaysia portal. Incomplete files are returned.

03

Evaluation

The project is evaluated using the NIA Scorecard against national objectives.

04

Approval

An approval-in-principle letter is issued detailing the tier and specific conditions.

05

Compliance

Performance is monitored annually against agreed commitments.

Key Deadlines & Mechanics

What Companies Must Note

Important Cut-off

PIA 1986 Applications Closed

The Government no longer accepts new manufacturing incentive applications under the Promotion of Investments Act 1986 after 28 February 2026.

Note: Existing approvals remain valid per their terms and conditions.

Global Minimum Tax

Malaysia and the OECD Pillar Two / GloBE Rules

Malaysia implements the Global Minimum Tax (GMT) from 2025, in line with the OECD/G20 Inclusive Framework’s Two-Pillar Solution. Under the GloBE Rules, a global minimum effective tax rate (ETR) of 15% applies, with a top-up tax charged where a group’s ETR in a jurisdiction falls below that level.

Companies with annual global revenue of at least EUR 750 million may be subject to top-up tax if their ETR in Malaysia is below 15%. This is collected through the Domestic Top-Up Tax (DTT) mechanism under Part XI of the Income Tax Act 1967, enforced from 1 January 2025.

The NIF is designed with this environment in mind — focusing on real economic substance and measurable national benefits rather than tax advantages alone.
