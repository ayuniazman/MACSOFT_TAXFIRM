Direct Tax

The essentials

What is
direct tax?

Direct tax is imposed directly on a taxpayer’s income, gains, property transactions or business activities. The tax that applies depends on the taxpayer and the relevant transaction or activity.

In Malaysia, direct taxes are generally administered by the Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia, also known as LHDN. The five categories below provide a starting point for understanding where an obligation may arise.

A closer look.

Understand what each tax covers, the governing legislation and the considerations commonly involved.

01 / Income & profits %

Income Tax

Income Tax Act 1967

Income tax is imposed on income earned by individuals and businesses in Malaysia, and is administered by LHDN. Tax payable depends on chargeable income, residency status and the applicable rates.

Resident individuals: 0–30% General corporate rate: 24%

Key point. Income tax treatment varies by taxpayer and type of income. Withholding tax on certain payments may also apply; its rate depends on the relevant rules and any applicable treaty.

Individual and corporate income tax

Resident individuals are generally taxed at progressive rates on chargeable income and may qualify for applicable reliefs. Non-resident individuals are subject to the rules and rates relevant to their status.

Companies are taxed on chargeable income; qualifying small and medium-sized companies may be subject to preferential treatment. Partnership income is considered under the rules applicable to partners rather than treating every partnership as a company.

Withholding tax

Certain payments to non-residents, including royalties, may give rise to withholding tax obligations. The applicable rate and treatment depend on the payment, domestic rules and any relevant Double Taxation Agreement.

Tax compliance and planning

Maintain appropriate records, submit required returns and consider available deductions, reliefs and incentives where the conditions are met. Transactions may also raise separate questions under Real Property Gains Tax or Capital Gains Tax; assess them against the relevant legislation and current guidance.

02 / Property gains ⌂

Real Property Gains Tax

Real Property Gains Tax Act 1976

RPGT applies to chargeable gains from the disposal of real property situated in Malaysia, including residential property, commercial buildings, land and other qualifying real property. The amount depends on the disposer category and holding period.

Key point. A Real Property Company (RPC) is generally identified using a 75% threshold relating to the value of its Malaysian real property or RPC shares against its total tangible assets, subject to statutory conditions.

How is RPGT determined?

The chargeable gain is considered by reference to acquisition and disposal prices, allowable expenses and applicable exemptions. The disposer’s category and holding period affect the treatment.

RPGT and disposal of company shares

Disposals of RPC shares can fall within RPGT rules. Capital Gains Tax rules may also be relevant to certain share disposals. The interaction depends on the disposer, asset and applicable conditions; consult current legislation and LHDN guidance.

Filing and payment requirements

Disposals may require RPGT filings with LHDN, and an acquirer may have an obligation to retain and remit part of the consideration. Check current filing, payment and exemption requirements for the specific transaction.

03 / Capital gains ↗

Capital Gains Tax

Income Tax Act 1967 (ITA 1967)

Capital Gains Tax (CGT) is imposed on gains or profits arising from the disposal of specified capital assets. In Malaysia, the regime primarily applies to companies, limited liability partnerships (LLPs), trust bodies and co-operative societies disposing of certain unlisted shares and other qualifying capital assets. It has been effective since 1 January 2024.

Key point. CGT does not apply to every capital asset or every taxpayer. Its application depends on the type and location of the asset, the taxpayer's category and the relevant statutory conditions. Individuals are generally outside the scope of this CGT regime.

What assets are subject to CGT?

The Malaysian CGT regime covers specific categories of capital assets, including:

  • Unlisted shares: Shares in companies incorporated in Malaysia that are not listed on a stock exchange.
  • Shares in relevant foreign companies: Shares in certain controlled companies incorporated outside Malaysia that derive value from Malaysian real property or shares in other controlled companies, subject to Section 15C of the ITA 1967.
  • Foreign capital assets: Gains from the disposal of capital assets situated outside Malaysia received in Malaysia by qualifying Malaysian resident entities, subject to the applicable rules and exemptions.
Who is subject to Capital Gains Tax?

CGT generally applies to the following categories of taxpayers when they dispose of assets covered by the legislation:

  • Companies
  • Limited liability partnerships (LLPs)
  • Trust bodies
  • Co-operative societies

Certain Labuan entities that elect or are subject to taxation under the Income Tax Act 1967 may also fall within the CGT regime.

The CGT rules do not generally impose this tax on individuals disposing of their personal capital assets.

CGT rates and exemptions

The applicable CGT rate depends on the type of capital asset, its acquisition date and the relevant tax provisions.

For qualifying disposals of unlisted shares, the applicable treatment generally includes:

  • Shares acquired before 1 January 2024: An option of 10% on chargeable gains or 2% on the gross disposal price, subject to the applicable rules.
  • Shares acquired on or after 1 January 2024: Generally 10% on chargeable gains.

Exemptions and special tax treatments may apply to qualifying transactions. Foreign capital assets received in Malaysia are also subject to separate exemption conditions. Eligibility should be assessed against the latest LHDN guidelines.

Filing and payment requirements

Taxpayers subject to CGT must submit the Capital Gains Tax Return Form (CGTRF), also known as BNCKM, through the MyTax portal using the e-CKM system.

The return and applicable tax payment must generally be submitted within 60 days from the date of disposal of the capital asset.

Taxpayers should maintain supporting documents, including acquisition records, disposal agreements, transaction values and relevant expenditure, to support their CGT calculations.

CGT vs Real Property Gains Tax (RPGT)

CGT and RPGT are separate tax regimes under different legislation. RPGT generally applies to gains from the disposal of Malaysian real property, while CGT applies to specified capital asset disposals under the Income Tax Act 1967.

Following the introduction of CGT, qualifying disposals of Real Property Company (RPC) shares by companies, LLPs, trust bodies and co-operative societies subject to the Income Tax Act 1967 fall under CGT rather than RPGT. However, certain Labuan entities carrying on business activities under Section 2B of the Labuan Business Activity Tax Act 1990 remain subject to RPGT for RPC share disposals.

The applicable tax treatment depends on the taxpayer, asset and relevant legislation.

04 / Petroleum operations ⌁

Petroleum Income Tax

Petroleum (Income Tax) Act 1967

Petroleum Income Tax concerns income from petroleum operations in Malaysia, including exploration, extraction, production and other activities covered by the legislation.

Key point. Petroleum operations are assessed under a dedicated framework, so the nature of the operations and contractual arrangements matter.

Tax computation and compliance

Accounts of profits or losses from petroleum operations support the determination of chargeable income. Specific rules and rates apply under the governing framework, and contractual arrangements can affect the analysis. File relevant returns and supporting documents with LHDN as required.

05 / Written instruments ▤

Stamp Duty

Stamp Act 1949

Stamp duty is imposed on specified legal, commercial and financial instruments. It generally applies to the written document, such as a property transfer, tenancy agreement, loan agreement or share transfer.

Key point. The duty depends on the instrument type, its consideration or value, and the applicable legislation.

Types of Stamp Duty

Ad valorem duty is linked to the relevant value or consideration, while fixed duty applies as a set amount to specified instruments. The instrument classification determines which approach applies.

Exemptions and reliefs

Reliefs or exemptions may be available for qualifying transfers or restructuring exercises. Eligibility can depend on statutory conditions and required approvals, so review the transaction before claiming relief.

06 / Labuan activities ⌂

Labuan Business Activity Tax

Labuan Business Activity Tax Act 1990 (LBATA)

This tax applies to chargeable profits of Labuan entities carrying out business activities in, from or through Labuan. Treatment distinguishes trading and non-trading activities and is subject to substance requirements.

Qualifying trading: 3% Qualifying non-trading: 0% Substance not met: 24%

Key point. Activity classification and compliance with applicable substance requirements are central to the tax treatment.

Labuan trading activities

Trading activities can include banking, insurance, trading, management and licensing carried on by a Labuan entity. The applicable treatment is subject to the relevant conditions.

Labuan non-trading activities

Non-trading activity generally concerns holding investments, subject to statutory classification and conditions. Intellectual property income should be considered separately under the applicable rules.

Substance requirements and tax compliance

Applicable substance requirements can involve employees, operating expenditure, and management and control in Labuan. Labuan entities should maintain appropriate records and submit required returns to LHDN.
