Transfer Pricing

01 / Introduction

What is transfer pricing?

Transfer pricing refers to the pricing of transactions involving goods, services, intangible assets and financial arrangements between associated persons or related companies within the same group.

Under Malaysia’s transfer pricing framework, controlled transactions must comply with the Arm’s Length Principle (ALP). This means that the prices and conditions should be consistent with those that independent parties would have agreed under comparable circumstances.

The principle helps prevent profits from being artificially shifted between related companies or tax jurisdictions through non-market pricing.

Current Malaysian framework
2023 – 2026

Malaysia’s transfer pricing rules are principally supported by Section 140A of the Income Tax Act 1967, the Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Rules 2023 and the Malaysia Transfer Pricing Guidelines 2024.

02 / Malaysian legal framework

How transfer pricing
is regulated in Malaysia

Malaysia’s transfer pricing framework has developed through legislation, rules, guidelines and tax audit guidance.

01

Income Tax Act 1967

Section 140A requires transactions between persons who are associated to be conducted at arm’s length. It gives the Director General of Inland Revenue powers to make adjustments where the conditions of a controlled transaction are not arm’s length.

02

Transfer Pricing Rules 2023

The Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Rules 2023 set out requirements for applying the arm’s length principle, including comparability analysis, transfer pricing methods and contemporaneous documentation.

03

Malaysia TP Guidelines 2024

The Malaysia Transfer Pricing Guidelines 2024 explain how taxpayers should apply the rules in practice, including delineating controlled transactions, selecting methods and preparing documentation.

04

Audit Framework 2025

The Transfer Pricing Tax Audit Framework 2025 provides the current administrative framework for HASiL transfer pricing audits and replaces the 2024 audit framework.

Important: The Malaysia Transfer Pricing Guidelines 2012 are no longer the current principal guideline. Use the 2024 Guidelines together with the 2023 Rules and subsequent amendments.
03 / International tax transparency

Country-by-country
reporting (CbCR)

CbCR requires certain large multinational enterprise (MNE) groups to report information about their income, taxes paid and indicators of economic activity across the jurisdictions in which they operate.

RM 3 billion

Consolidated group revenue threshold
Malaysia’s CbCR requirements generally apply to an MNE group where its consolidated group revenue in the financial year preceding the reporting financial year is at least RM3 billion, subject to the Malaysian entity and reporting-entity conditions in the Rules.

Who may be required to report?

Not every company is required to file a CbC report.

The rules apply to qualifying MNE groups with a Malaysian reporting entity or other Malaysian constituent entity/establishment within the scope of the CbCR legislation. The reporting entity is generally the ultimate holding entity (UHE), or a surrogate holding entity in specified circumstances.

For the precise tests, including the treatment of permanent establishments and Labuan entities, refer to the current HASiL CbCR guidance and the Income Tax (Country-by-Country Reporting) Rules 2016, as amended.

NotificationOn or before reporting FY-end
CbC report filingWithin 12 months after FY-end

Key CbCR timeline

01 / Notification

By financial year-end

A Malaysian constituent entity must provide the required notification, including the identity and tax residence of the reporting entity where applicable, on or before the last day of the reporting financial year.

02 / Preparation

Reporting financial year

The reporting entity prepares the CbC report for the relevant reporting financial year in accordance with the CbCR Rules.

03 / Filing

Within 12 months

The CbC report is generally due no later than 12 months after the last day of the reporting financial year.

CbCR is a separate reporting obligation from transfer pricing documentation. The RM3 billion threshold relates to CbCR and should not be treated as a general exemption from transfer pricing requirements.

04 / Transfer pricing methods

Five recognised
methods.

The method selected should be the most appropriate method for the controlled transaction, taking into account the facts, functions performed, assets used, risks assumed and availability of reliable comparable data.

Traditional transaction method

Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP)

Compares the price charged in a controlled transaction with the price charged in a comparable transaction between independent parties.

Simple exampleA company sells the same product to both a related company and an independent customer. The independent-party price may provide a direct comparison, after appropriate adjustments for differences.

05 / Compliance

Transfer pricing
documentation

Documentation helps a taxpayer demonstrate that its controlled transactions comply with the arm’s length principle.

FULL DOCUMENTATION

Contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation (CTPD)

Where the full documentation requirements apply, the file generally includes information about the group and taxpayer, business and industry, controlled transactions, functional analysis, comparability analysis, selected method and supporting financial information.

SIMPLIFIED REQUIREMENTS

Minimum documentation

Taxpayers who qualify under the relevant provisions may prepare minimum documentation rather than the full documentation. The applicable conditions and required contents must be checked against the current Rules and Guidelines.

EXEMPTIONS

Check the applicable exemption

Specific categories of transactions or taxpayers may qualify for exemptions from preparing full CTPD, subject to the conditions in the Rules. Exemptions are not automatic and should be assessed against the relevant criteria.

14-day furnishing requirement: If HASiL issues a written notice requesting CTPD, the taxpayer must furnish the documentation within 14 days. Failure to comply may lead to action under Section 113B of the Income Tax Act 1967.
06 / Tax administration

Transfer pricing audits
and consequences

HASiL may review controlled transactions and make adjustments where the pricing or conditions are not consistent with the arm’s length principle.

01

Review and information request

During an audit, HASiL may request records, explanations, agreements, calculations and transfer pricing documentation relevant to the controlled transactions.

02

Transfer pricing adjustment

Where the controlled transaction is not arm’s length, the Director General may make an adjustment under Section 140A to reflect an arm’s length outcome.

03

Surcharge and penalties

A surcharge of up to 5% may be imposed on the amount of transfer pricing adjustment under Section 140A(3C). Separate penalties may apply for failure to comply with documentation requirements, including Section 113B.

The actual tax consequences depend on the facts, applicable legislation and current HASiL audit guidance. The surcharge is not necessarily 5% in every case.

07 / Latest developments

Recent Malaysian
transfer pricing updates

Key developments to reflect in a current overview of Malaysia’s transfer pricing regime.

24 DEC 2024

Malaysia Transfer Pricing Guidelines 2024

The 2024 Guidelines replaced the 2012 Guidelines and apply from Year of Assessment 2023 onwards.

31 JUL 2025

Transfer Pricing Tax Audit Framework 2025

HASiL published the 2025 audit framework, which supersedes the 2024 framework and updates the administrative approach to transfer pricing audits.

30 JUL 2026

Guidelines on controlled financial transactions: intra-group loans

HASiL published guidance addressing controlled financial transactions involving intra-group loans, including the analysis of debt characteristics and arm’s length interest.

27 AUG 2026

Transfer Pricing (Amendment) Rules 2026

The amendment includes changes to the definition of an MNE Group and provides for an offsetting adjustment mechanism, subject to a request and the Director General’s approval. It is deemed effective from Year of Assessment 2023.

For businesses with intercompany loans: Review the 2026 guidance on controlled financial transactions and assess whether existing loan arrangements and interest terms are supported by an appropriate arm’s length analysis.
