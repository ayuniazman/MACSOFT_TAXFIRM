01 / Introduction

What is transfer pricing?

Transfer pricing refers to intercompany pricing arrangements for goods, services and intangibles between associated persons.

Ideally, transfer prices should not differ from prevailing market prices between independent persons. However, business transactions between associated persons may not always reflect market forces.

Malaysia transfer pricing guidelines
20 July 2012

The Inland Revenue Board Malaysia published Transfer Pricing Guidelines on this date. The guidelines provide guidance on the law and rules for controlled transactions, methods and manner, administrative requirements, and records and documentation.

02 / Reporting requirements

Country-by-country reporting

Country-by-country reporting (CbCR) is governed by the Income Tax (Country-by-Country Reporting) Rules 2016, effective from 1 January 2017. The conditions below follow the supplied original content.

MYR 3 billion

Consolidated group revenue threshold
One of the conditions is that total consolidated group revenue in the preceding financial year is at least MYR 3 billion.

When do the stated conditions apply?

01

Cross-border transactions

Any constituent entity enters into cross-border transactions with another constituent entity.

02

Group revenue

Total consolidated group revenue in the preceding financial year is at least MYR 3 billion.

03

Ultimate holding company

The ultimate holding company is incorporated under the Companies Act 1965 or written law and is resident in Malaysia.

04

Constituent entities

Constituent entities are incorporated or registered under the Companies Act 1965, any written law, or the laws of a territory outside Malaysia, and are resident in Malaysia.

Who must file and notify?

The ultimate holding company of a multinational enterprise (MNC) group that is resident in Malaysia must file the CbC report with the Director General of Inland Revenue. The report must comply with the Rules and cover the reporting financial year on or before the specified date.
A surrogate holding company files instead if any of the following circumstances applies:
  1. The UHC is not resident in Malaysia and is not required to file a CbC report in its jurisdiction.
  2. The UHC's jurisdiction of tax residence has an international agreement with Malaysia but is not a party to the Multilateral Competent Authority Agreement on Exchange of CbC Reports at the time of filing.
  3. There is a systemic failure of the UHC's jurisdiction of tax residence, and the Director General has notified the Malaysian constituent entity of that failure.
On or before the last day of the reporting financial year, a Malaysian constituent entity must notify the Director General in writing if it is the UHC or surrogate holding company.
If a constituent entity is not the reporting entity, it must notify the Director General in writing of the identity and tax residence of the reporting entity on or before the last day of the reporting financial year.

Key reporting timeline

Notification 01

By financial year-end

The Malaysian constituent entity provides written notification of its status or of the reporting entity's identity and tax residence, as applicable, on or before the last day of the reporting financial year.

Filing 02

Within 12 months

The CbC report is due no later than 12 months after the last day of the reporting financial year.

Report 03

Reporting financial year

The report must comply with the Rules and cover the reporting financial year.
