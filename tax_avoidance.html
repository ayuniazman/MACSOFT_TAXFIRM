TAX KNOWLEDGE

What is tax avoidance?

Tax avoidance means arranging your finances within the law so you pay no more tax than you actually owe. It is legal, and it is different from tax evasion.

The simple definition

Tax avoidance is using the reliefs, deductions, rebates and allowances that the tax law offers. The law is written so that people who save for retirement, support their families or invest in their business can pay less tax. Using those provisions is not a loophole. It is how the system is meant to work.

Rule of thumb: if you report everything honestly and claim only what the law allows, you are avoiding tax legally.

Common legal examples in Malaysia

  • Claiming individual tax reliefs, such as EPF contributions and life insurance
  • Claiming relief for education fees, medical expenses and lifestyle spending, within the limits set each year
  • Contributing to approved savings schemes that qualify for relief
  • Making approved donations that are deductible
  • For businesses, claiming capital allowances on equipment and deducting allowable business expenses

Avoidance vs evasion

The two are often confused, but they sit on opposite sides of the law.

Tax avoidance

Legal. You use reliefs and allowances the law provides, report all your income, and keep records to support every claim.

Tax evasion

Illegal. It involves hiding income, inventing deductions or falsifying records. It can lead to heavy penalties, prosecution and imprisonment.

When avoidance goes too far

Not every arrangement that reduces tax is accepted. An arrangement that is artificial, contrived or has little real commercial purpose, and exists mainly to gain a tax advantage Parliament never intended, can be challenged even if it looks legal on paper.

The Income Tax Act 1967 has anti-avoidance rules for this. Section 140 lets the Director General of Inland Revenue disregard such arrangements. Section 140A covers price adjustments and disallowed interest on certain transactions, and Section 141 deals with dealings between residents and non-residents.

If Section 140 applies, the Director General can:

  • Treat one person's income as another person's income
  • Revise or impose a person's tax liability
  • Issue an assessment or additional assessment
  • Cancel a right to a tax refund, or require a refund to be repaid

How to stay on the right side

  • Report all of your income
  • Keep receipts and documents for every claim
  • Claim only reliefs you genuinely qualify for
  • Ask a tax professional before using a complex structure

Good tax planning follows both the wording and the intent of the law. Every transaction should have a genuine commercial reason, and reducing tax should not be one of its main purposes.

Common questions

Is tax avoidance legal in Malaysia?

Yes, when you use reliefs and allowances the law gives you and report your income honestly. It becomes a problem only when an arrangement has no real purpose other than reducing tax.

Is claiming tax relief the same as avoidance?

Yes. Claiming relief you are entitled to is the most common form of legal tax avoidance.

What records should I keep?

Keep receipts, invoices, statements and certificates that support each relief or deduction you claim, in case the IRBM asks for them.
