Indirect Tax

The essentials

What is
indirect tax?

Indirect tax is imposed on the consumption, purchase, importation or use of certain goods and services. Unlike direct taxes, which are generally imposed on the income or profits of taxpayers, indirect taxes are commonly collected by businesses from customers or accounted for by the relevant parties.

In Malaysia, indirect taxes are primarily administered by the Royal Malaysian Customs Department, also known as RMCD (Jabatan Kastam Diraja Malaysia). The four categories below provide an overview of the main indirect taxes and related consumption tax obligations.

01 / Sales & services ▤

Sales and Service Tax (SST)

Sales Tax Act 2018 & Service Tax Act 2018

Sales and Service Tax (SST) is Malaysia's indirect tax system implemented on 1 September 2018, replacing the Goods and Services Tax (GST). It consists of two separate taxes: Sales Tax and Service Tax, each governed by its respective legislation.

Sales Tax is a single-stage tax imposed on taxable goods manufactured in Malaysia and taxable goods imported into the country. Service Tax applies to prescribed taxable services provided by registered persons in the course of business. It also covers imported taxable services under the applicable provisions.

Key point. Sales Tax and Service Tax have different registration requirements, taxable categories and applicable rates. Businesses should assess their activities separately to determine their SST obligations.

Sales Tax and Service Tax

Sales Tax is generally imposed once in the supply chain on taxable goods manufactured locally by registered manufacturers or imported into Malaysia, subject to applicable exemptions.

Service Tax applies to prescribed taxable services provided by registered persons. The scope includes various service categories specified under the Service Tax Regulations.

The applicable tax treatment depends on the nature of the goods or services, the relevant legislation and any available exemptions.

Registration and threshold

Businesses must register for SST when they meet the prescribed registration requirements under the relevant legislation.

For Sales Tax, manufacturers of taxable goods generally have a registration threshold of RM500,000 in sales value over a 12-month period, subject to applicable conditions and exemptions.

For Service Tax, registration thresholds vary depending on the category of taxable services provided. Businesses exceeding the applicable threshold are required to register with RMCD.

Registered businesses must comply with SST requirements, including issuing appropriate invoices, maintaining records, submitting tax returns and making payments within the prescribed periods.

Tax rates and exemptions

Sales Tax rates depend on the classification of the taxable goods. Different goods may be subject to different rates, including specific rates or exemptions.

Service Tax rates depend on the category of taxable services. The general rate is 8%, while certain prescribed services remain subject to a 6% rate or other applicable treatment.

Exemptions may apply to qualifying goods, services or transactions. Businesses should refer to the latest Sales Tax and Service Tax Orders and RMCD guidance when determining the correct treatment.

02 / Selected goods ◈

Excise Duty

Excise Act 1976

Excise Duty is an indirect tax imposed on specific goods manufactured in Malaysia or imported into the country. It generally applies to selected products such as alcoholic beverages, tobacco products and motor vehicles.

The goods commonly subject to excise duty include:

01 / Liquor and alcoholic beverages 02 / Cigarettes and tobacco products 03 / Motor vehicles 04 / Playing cards 05 / Mahjong tiles

Key point. Excise Duty rates vary according to the type, classification and characteristics of the goods. Businesses involved in the manufacture, importation or movement of excisable goods must comply with the relevant customs requirements.

Goods subject to Excise Duty

Excise Duty applies to goods specified under the relevant excise legislation and orders. These include selected alcoholic beverages, tobacco products, motor vehicles, playing cards and mahjong tiles.

The applicable duty depends on the product's tariff classification and the relevant statutory provisions.

Manufacturing and importation

Locally manufactured excisable goods are subject to the relevant licensing, declaration and duty payment requirements.

Imported excisable goods may also be subject to excise duty upon importation, in addition to any applicable customs duty and sales tax.

Importers and manufacturers should ensure that the required declarations and supporting documentation are submitted to RMCD.

Duty assessment and compliance

The amount of excise duty payable depends on the applicable duty rate and the relevant measurement or value of the goods.

Businesses must comply with applicable licensing, record-keeping, declaration and payment requirements under the Excise Act 1976.

03 / International trade ⇄

Customs Duty

Customs Act 1967 & Customs Duties Order

Customs Duty is a tax imposed on certain goods imported into or exported from Malaysia. It is administered by RMCD and serves as a source of government revenue while also supporting the regulation of international trade and protection of domestic industries.

The applicable duty depends on the classification of the goods, their country of origin, customs value and the relevant preferential trade arrangements or exemptions.

Import Duty: Goods imported into Malaysia Export Duty: Selected goods exported from Malaysia

Key point. Not all imported or exported goods are subject to the same duty rates. Correct tariff classification and customs valuation are essential when determining the applicable customs duty.

Types of Customs Duty

Import Duty is imposed on specified goods brought into Malaysia. The applicable rate depends on the tariff classification and relevant customs orders.

Export Duty applies to specified goods exported from Malaysia. It is generally imposed on selected commodities and products rather than all exported goods.

Tariff classification and customs valuation

Goods must be classified according to the applicable tariff nomenclature. This classification determines the relevant customs duty rate and may also affect other import requirements.

Customs valuation establishes the value used for calculating applicable ad valorem duties. Importers should ensure that declarations accurately reflect the transaction and relevant valuation rules.

Preferential tariffs and exemptions

Malaysia participates in various free trade agreements that may provide preferential customs duty rates for qualifying goods originating from participating countries.

Businesses may also qualify for certain exemptions or facilities, subject to the relevant conditions, supporting documents and approvals.

04 / Digital economy ⌘

Service Tax on Digital Services (SToDS)

Service Tax Act 2018

Service Tax on Digital Services (SToDS) is an indirect tax imposed on digital services provided by foreign service providers to consumers in Malaysia. Introduced on 1 January 2020, it applies to qualifying services delivered through the internet or electronic networks, where the delivery is essentially automated and requires information technology.

Examples of digital services include online streaming platforms, software subscriptions, mobile applications, digital advertising and online platforms.

8% / Service Tax Rate RM500,000 / Registration Threshold Quarterly / Taxable Period

Key point. Foreign service providers exceeding the prescribed registration threshold must register with RMCD as Foreign Registered Persons (FRPs) and comply with the applicable digital service tax requirements.

Examples of taxable digital services

Digital services may include:

  • Online streaming and subscription services
  • Software and cloud-based services
  • Mobile applications and digital content
  • Online advertising services
  • Digital platforms and online marketplaces

Whether a particular service is taxable depends on its classification under the Service Tax Act 2018 and the applicable regulations and guidance.

Registration and threshold

Foreign service providers are required to register with RMCD as Foreign Registered Persons (FRPs) when the total value of digital services provided to consumers in Malaysia exceeds or is expected to exceed RM500,000 within a 12-month period.

A consumer is generally considered to be in Malaysia when at least two of the following conditions are fulfilled:

  • Payment is made using a credit or debit facility provided by a Malaysian financial institution or company.
  • The digital service is accessed using a Malaysian IP address or Malaysian international mobile country code.
  • The consumer resides in Malaysia.
Taxable period and payment

The taxable period for a Foreign Registered Person is three months (quarterly).

Service tax becomes due when payment for the digital service is received, unless approval has been obtained from the Director General to account for tax based on the invoice date.

The service tax return and payment must be submitted to RMCD by the last day of the month following the end of the taxable period.
