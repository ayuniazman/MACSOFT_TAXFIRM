An overview of withholding tax under Malaysia's Income Tax Act 1967
01 / Overview

What is withholding tax?

Withholding tax is an amount deducted by the party making a payment (the payer) from certain income payable to a non-resident (the payee). The tax is then remitted to the Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia (LHDN).

Under the Income Tax Act 1967, a payer making a payment covered by the relevant provisions must deduct tax at the prescribed rate and remit it to the Director General of Inland Revenue within the applicable deadline.

The payer

Who makes the payment?

Generally, a person or entity carrying on a business in Malaysia that makes a payment subject to withholding tax to a non-resident payee.

The payee

Who receives the payment?

A non-resident individual or entity receiving income covered by the relevant withholding tax provisions.

02 / Key obligation

Understand the payment process.

Withholding tax involves identifying a covered payment, applying the appropriate rules and remitting the tax within the statutory time limit.

STEP 01

Identify

Determine whether the payment to the non-resident falls within a withholding tax provision.

STEP 02

Deduct

Apply the prescribed rate, taking into account the applicable domestic rules and any relevant treaty relief.

STEP 03

Remit

Submit the withholding tax to the Director General of Inland Revenue within the applicable statutory deadline.

Important deadline

The general rule described in Section 109 of the Income Tax Act 1967 requires payment within one month after the relevant payment is paid or credited. Other withholding tax provisions have their own requirements. Confirm the applicable section and deadline for your case.

03 / Payment categories

Which payments may be subject to withholding tax?

Explore the categories below. Search by payment type or expand a card to see a brief explanation.

9 categories
01 / CONTRACTS

Contract Payments

Payments under qualifying contracts involving non-residents.

Contract payments may be subject to the rules in Section 107A. The treatment depends on the nature of the contract and the payment.
02 / FINANCING

Interest

Interest payments to non-residents under applicable withholding tax provisions.

Interest may fall under Section 109, subject to statutory exemptions, applicable conditions and any relevant double taxation agreement.
03 / INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

Royalties

Payments for rights to use certain intellectual property or other qualifying assets.

Royalty payments may be subject to Section 109. Classification depends on the rights granted and the applicable statutory definition.
04 / SPECIAL CLASSES

Special Classes of Income

Certain technical fees, services and payments for the use of movable property.

Section 109B may apply to income within Section 4A, including qualifying technical or management services and payments for the use of movable property. The specific conditions must be checked.
05 / FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS

Interest Paid by Approved Financial Institutions

Interest covered by the relevant provisions, subject to applicable exclusions and exemptions.

The relevant rules distinguish qualifying interest from exempt interest. Confirm the specific provision, recipient and exemption conditions before applying tax.
06 / ENTERTAINMENT

Non-Resident Public Entertainers

Qualifying income earned by non-resident public entertainers in Malaysia.

Income of non-resident public entertainers is subject to specific provisions, including Section 109A. Its rules and deadlines should be considered separately.
07 / INVESTMENTS

Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)

Distributions involving different categories of recipients.

The treatment of REIT distributions depends on the recipient's classification and the applicable tax provisions. The categories in the supplied overview include resident companies, non-resident companies and foreign institutional investors.
08 / FUNDS & TAKAFUL

Family Funds, Takaful Family Funds & Dana Am

Fund-related income with treatment depending on the recipient's category.

Recipient categories in the supplied overview include individuals and other recipients, as well as non-resident companies. Confirm the precise fund classification and governing provision.
09 / OTHER INCOME

Income Under Section 4(f)

Other gains or profits falling within Section 4(f) of the Income Tax Act 1967.

Section 4(f) concerns gains or profits not falling within the other specified paragraphs of Section 4. Whether withholding tax applies depends on the applicable statutory provisions and facts.

Note: These cards are introductory summaries, not a determination that every payment in a category is taxable. Rates, exemptions and conditions depend on the applicable legislation and circumstances.
