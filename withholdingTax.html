Withholding Tax

01 / Overview

What is withholding tax?

Withholding tax is an amount deducted by a payer from certain payments made to a recipient, as required under the Income Tax Act 1967 (ITA). The deducted amount must be remitted to the Inland Revenue Board of Malaysia (HASiL) within the prescribed period. The withholding tax obligation depends on the nature of the payment, the recipient's tax residency status and the applicable provisions of the ITA.

Under the Income Tax Act 1967, a payer making a payment covered by the relevant provisions must deduct tax at the prescribed rate and remit it to the Director General of Inland Revenue within the applicable deadline.

The payer

Who makes the payment?

Generally, a person or entity carrying on a business in Malaysia that makes a payment subject to withholding tax to a non-resident payee.

The payee

Who receives the payment?

A non-resident individual or entity receiving income covered by the relevant withholding tax provisions.

02 / Key obligation

Understand the payment process.

Withholding tax involves identifying a covered payment, applying the appropriate rules and remitting the tax within the statutory time limit.

STEP 01

Identify

Determine whether the payment to the non-resident falls within a withholding tax provision.

STEP 02

Deduct

Apply the prescribed rate, taking into account the applicable domestic rules and any relevant treaty relief.

STEP 03

Remit

Submit the withholding tax to the Director General of Inland Revenue within the applicable statutory deadline.

Important deadline

Withholding tax must generally be remitted to HASiL within one month after the payment is paid or credited to the recipient, as applicable under the relevant provision of the Income Tax Act 1967. Payers should identify the applicable withholding tax provision and ensure that the deduction and remittance are completed within the required period.

03 / Payment categories

Which payments may be subject to withholding tax?

Explore the categories below. Search by payment type or expand a card to see a brief explanation.

9 categories
01 / CONTRACTS

Contract Payments

Payments under qualifying contracts involving non-residents.

Under Section 107A of the Income Tax Act 1967, payments to non-resident contractors for qualifying services performed in Malaysia are subject to withholding tax. The payer must deduct 10% from the contract payment on account of the contractor's tax liability and an additional 3% on account of the tax liability of the contractor's employees.
02 / FINANCING

Interest

Interest payments to non-residents under applicable withholding tax provisions.

Interest paid to a non-resident recipient is generally subject to 15% withholding tax on the gross amount. However, certain exemptions may apply, and a reduced rate may be available under an applicable Double Taxation Agreement (DTA).
03 / INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

Royalties

Payments for rights to use certain intellectual property or other qualifying assets.

Royalty payments to non-residents are generally subject to 10% withholding tax on the gross amount. Examples include payments for the use of copyrights, software, patents, trademarks, designs and technical know-how. The classification of a payment depends on the rights granted and the applicable tax rules.
04 / SPECIAL CLASSES

Special Classes of Income

Certain technical fees, services and payments for the use of movable property.

Special classes of income under Section 4A of the Income Tax Act 1967 include certain services relating to the use of property or rights, management or administration, and payments for the use of movable property. Qualifying payments to non-residents are generally subject to 10% withholding tax. For relevant service categories, the amount attributable to services performed in Malaysia is subject to the withholding tax rules.
05 / FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS

Interest Paid by Approved Financial Institutions

Interest covered by the relevant provisions, subject to applicable exclusions and exemptions.

The relevant rules distinguish qualifying interest from exempt interest. Confirm the specific provision, recipient and exemption conditions before applying tax.
06 / ENTERTAINMENT

Non-Resident Public Entertainers

Qualifying income earned by non-resident public entertainers in Malaysia.

Income of non-resident public entertainers is subject to specific provisions, including Section 109A. Its rules and deadlines should be considered separately.
07 / INVESTMENTS

Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)

Distributions involving different categories of recipients.

The treatment of REIT distributions depends on the recipient's classification and the applicable tax provisions. The categories in the supplied overview include resident companies, non-resident companies and foreign institutional investors.
08 / FUNDS & TAKAFUL

Family Funds, Takaful Family Funds & Dana Am

Fund-related income with treatment depending on the recipient's category.

Recipient categories in the supplied overview include individuals and other recipients, as well as non-resident companies. Confirm the precise fund classification and governing provision.
09 / OTHER INCOME

Income Under Section 4(f)

Other gains or profits falling within Section 4(f) of the Income Tax Act 1967.

Certain income classified under paragraph 4(f) of the Income Tax Act 1967 is subject to 10% withholding tax when paid to a non-resident. This category covers qualifying gains or profits that do not fall under the other specified income categories. The nature and source of the income must be examined to determine whether withholding tax applies.
10 / OTHER INCOME

Early Withdrawal from Deferred Annuity or Private Retirement Scheme (PRS)

Qualifying early withdrawals from deferred annuity arrangements or Private Retirement Schemes (PRS) before the individual reaches 55 years old.

An 8% withholding tax applies to qualifying withdrawals made before an individual reaches 55 years old. However, certain withdrawals are excluded, including those due to permanent disability, serious illness, mental disability, death, permanent departure from Malaysia and qualifying healthcare expenses.

Note: These cards are introductory summaries, not a determination that every payment in a category is taxable. Rates, exemptions and conditions depend on the applicable legislation and circumstances.
