Income Tax Audit & Investigation

The Core Difference

Audit vs. Investigation

A Tax Audit is generally a routine review of submitted records to verify accuracy and encourage voluntary compliance. It does not imply you have done anything wrong.

A Tax Investigation, on the other hand, is a more detailed examination conducted when tax evasion or serious discrepancies are suspected. It involves gathering evidence of deliberate non-compliance and may lead to prosecution.

Tax Audit Verifies compliance. Usually resolved via correspondence or interviews.
Tax Investigation Gathers evidence of fraud/evasion. Can involve surprise visits and asset freezing.
Aim of Tax Audit

Objectives of a Tax Audit

01

Promote Voluntary Compliance

Encourage taxpayers to comply voluntarily with the Self-Assessment System (SAS) and the Income Tax Act 1967.

02

Educate Taxpayers

Raise awareness and help taxpayers understand their rights and responsibilities under current tax laws.

03

Prevent Revenue Leakage

Identify errors, omissions, and non-compliance through the proper enforcement of tax laws to protect national revenue.

04

Ensure Accurate Reporting

Verify that income is properly reported and the correct amount of tax is paid to the government.

How IRBM Reviews

Methods of Audit Review

01

Desk Audit

A document-based review carried out at the IRBM office. It generally focuses on specific information declared in the return.

  • Conducted at an IRBM office
  • Involves correspondence with the taxpayer
  • Resolves issues related to income adjustments
  • Reviews compliance with employer responsibilities
02

Field Audit

A broader review involving interviews on the business's modus operandi, conducted at the taxpayer's premises or agreed locations.

  • Examines records related to income and expenses
  • Reviews agreements (substance over form)
  • Verifies compliance with approved incentive claims
  • Checks withholding tax deductions and MTD/CP38 remittances
Risk Indicators

What Triggers a Tax Audit?

  • Large fluctuations in revenue or margins year-on-year
  • Recurring losses or thin margins compared to industry peers
  • Inconsistencies between tax returns and third-party data (e.g., CP58, customs data)
  • Related-party transactions without robust Transfer Pricing documentation
  • Aggressive tax positions unsupported by public rulings
  • Participation in cash-heavy sectors or unusual cash movements
Penalties

Consequences of Non-Compliance

Audit

Tiered Penalties (Under Section 113(2))

  • First offence: 15%
  • Second offence: 30%
  • Third and subsequent: 45%

Note: No penalties apply for technical adjustments (reasonable differences in legal interpretation). Voluntary disclosures carry lower rates (10% if within 6 months, 15% otherwise).

Investigation

Heavy Penalties & Prosecution

For intentional incorrect returns or tax evasion, the statutory penalty rate is 100%.

  • Potential court prosecution
  • Fines and imprisonment under Section 114
  • Assets may be frozen, seized, or forfeited under AMLATFPUAA
  • No time limit for assessments involving fraud or wilful evasion
Risk Mitigation

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

01

Weak Record Keeping

Missing source documents, unreconciled sales vs. banking, and undocumented entries invite adjustments.

Fix this issue +
Perform monthly monitoring and maintain complete reconciliations.
02

Transfer Pricing (TP) Blind Spots

Domestic and cross-border related-party dealings without contemporaneous TP documentation.

Fix this issue +
Ensure TP files (master/local) and benchmarking are current and aligned with IRB guidelines.
03

Employment Tax & Benefits

Misclassification of allowances or shadow payroll gaps for secondees.

Fix this issue +
Use IRB public rulings for benefit valuation and align HR, payroll, and finance data.
04

Capital vs. Revenue Misclassification

Over-claiming capital allowances or treating capital items as deductible expenses.

Fix this issue +
Maintain a consistent fixed asset register (FAR) and clear policy notes.
